---
title: How Kite works
description: The pieces of a Kite deployment and the life of a payment.
---

Kite is a single server. It holds your configuration and history in Postgres, watches the chains you enable, and settles payments through Kite contracts. Your customers pay your server, and the funds go where you say. You can run the server yourself, or use [Kite's hosted instances](/hosted).

## The pieces

| Piece               | What it does                                                                                                           |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **API**             | Everything Kite does, over REST under `/api`. Documented in the [API Reference](/api-reference).                      |
| **Dashboard**       | A web app for running the server: chains, tokens, apps, keys, payment links, transfers. Sign in with the root key.     |
| **Checkout**        | The page customers pay on, at `/pay/<payment link id>`. Agents pay the same link over [x402](/payments/x402).          |
| **Operator wallet** | A wallet derived from your mnemonic. It sends the transactions that settle and refund payments.                        |
| **Kite contracts**  | A factory and one contract per payment. See [Contracts](/contracts/overview).                                         |
| **Providers**       | Optional services that report deposits, pay the operator's gas and deliver payouts. See [Providers](/server/providers). |
| **Postgres**        | Configuration, apps, payment links, quotes, transfers, events and webhook deliveries.                                  |

## Apps

A Kite server can serve several businesses or projects, each as an **app**. Payment links, customers, API keys, webhooks, fees, quotes and transfers all belong to an app, and an app's API keys only see its own data.

Chains, tokens and price feeds are shared by the whole server. Each app turns on the chains it accepts, with an address on each where its payments settle. It can also have every payment [paid out](/payments/payouts) as one token on one chain. See [Apps](/payments/apps).

## The life of a payment

1. **A payment link exists.** You create it in the dashboard, or your backend creates one per order with the [SDK](/integrate/sdk). It has a title, an amount in a currency such as USD, and optionally line items and your own `reference`.
2. **The customer opens checkout** and picks how to pay: a token on one of the chains the app accepts, from their wallet or by sending it.
3. **Kite quotes it.** The amount is converted into the chosen token at a live rate. A quote holds for 5 minutes.
4. **A transfer opens.** Kite works out the payment's own contract address from the transfer's terms (who's paid, how much, fees, refund address, expiry) before anything is deployed. That address is where the customer pays. If the app has a payout, the payment's recipient is a NEAR Intents deposit address quoted now.
5. **The customer pays.** They send the tokens to that address, from a connected wallet in one step or from any wallet or exchange. An agent that requests the link over HTTP pays with an [x402](/payments/x402) authorisation instead, without gas.
6. **Kite settles.** When the deposit lands, the operator deploys the contract and settles in one transaction: swap if needed, pay fees, and send the rest to the app's address on that chain, or into NEAR Intents for the payout. Overpayments go back to the customer.
7. **You hear about it.** Each status change is an event, delivered to your [webhooks](/integrate/webhooks) and shown on the transfer's timeline.

If the customer never pays, the transfer expires and is cancelled. If something goes wrong after they pay, Kite refunds them. See [Transfers](/payments/transfers) for every status.
